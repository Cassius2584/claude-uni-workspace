## The three tiers
| Tier | What you must be able to do | How to practise |
|---|---|---|
| **A: Reproduce** | Write it out in full from memory | Key-idea card daily, and **write it out on paper once a week** |
| **B: Reconstruct** | State the key idea in one sentence and rebuild it from that | Key-idea card daily, and occasionally rebuild it from the idea alone |
| **C: Statement only** | State it exactly and use it | Statement cloze card only |

**Test for "do I understand it":** close the notes, write the key idea in one sentence, then rebuild the whole

## How to assign a tier
- **A:** short (about half a page or less), standard, or the same technique as something a past paper asked for.
  Also any result the rest of the course depends on (e.g. Euler's theorem, Euler's criterion).
- **B:** medium or long, where the structure matters more than the exact lines. Also the famous theorem whose full
  proof is long (e.g. quadratic reciprocity).
- **C:** no proof in the notes, "left as reading" or "for keen students", or **marked non-examinable**.
- When unsure between A and B, pick B and say "aim for A" if the result is central to the course.
